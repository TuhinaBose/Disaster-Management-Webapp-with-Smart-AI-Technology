Disaster Safety Guidelines & Preparedness

Step-by-step life preservation protocols, before-during-after safety instructions, and verified survival equipment checklists approved by disaster management authorities.

PHASE 1
BEFORE a Flood

Preparation before water levels rise saves lives and keeps communications open.

  • Know your region's flood elevation zone and identify the highest surrounding topography.
  • Elevate all critical appliances, circuit breakers, and electrical wiring above flood baselines.
  • Keep your 72-hour survival kit, water purification tablets, and sealed medication ready.
  • Clear neighborhood drains and secure toxic chemicals out of water reach.
PHASE 2
DURING a Flood

Immediate survival actions when inundation begins.

  • Turn Around, Don't Drown: Just 6 inches of moving water can knock down an adult; 12 inches can sweep away cars.
  • Evacuate immediately if instructed by local authorities or emergency sirens.
  • Never touch submerged electrical equipment or severed power lines.
  • If trapped in a structure, move to the roof, not a closed attic; wave bright cloth for helicopter rescue.
PHASE 3
AFTER a Flood

Safe recovery and health prevention once waters recede.

  • Return home only after authorities issue an official all-clear bulletin.
  • Boil or chemically treat all municipal water before drinking to prevent cholera and leptospirosis.
  • Take photographs of property damage before cleanup for emergency relief assistance.
  • Inspect walls and foundations for structural integrity before entering buildings.

PHASE 1
BEFORE an Earthquake

Mitigation measures within your home or workspace.

  • Anchor tall bookcases, heavy mirrors, cabinets, and water heaters securely to structural wall studs.
  • Identify safe zones in every room: under sturdy wooden desks or against interior load-bearing walls.
  • Keep a flashlight, whistle, and thick-soled shoes next to every bed.
  • Learn how to manually shut off gas, water mains, and circuit breakers.
PHASE 2
DURING an Earthquake

Life-saving actions during severe seismic tremors.

  • DROP, COVER, AND HOLD ON: Drop to your hands and knees. Cover head and neck under a sturdy table. Hold on until shaking stops.
  • Stay indoors until ground movement stops. Never rush outside during shaking as falling glass kills.
  • If outdoors, move into open spaces away from high-rise buildings, utility lines, and facades.
  • If driving, pull over safely away from overpasses and remain inside with seatbelts fastened.
PHASE 3
AFTER an Earthquake

Inspection and aftershock management.

  • Anticipate seismic aftershocks. Be ready to repeat Drop, Cover, and Hold on.
  • Check for gas leaks (smell of rotten eggs). Do not strike matches or turn on light switches.
  • Wear thick shoes to avoid injuries from shattered glass and collapsed masonry.
  • Use text messages rather than phone calls to keep emergency networks unburdened.
Trapped Under Debris Protocol

Cover your mouth with a cloth to filter dust. Do not shout continuously to avoid dust inhalation and stamina loss. Instead, tap rhythmically with a metal object or rock against a pipe or wall so acoustic search sensors can triangulate your position.

PHASE 1
BEFORE a Cyclone
  • Inspect roof sheets, tiles, and window shutters; bolt them firmly against hurricane-force winds.
  • Prune dead tree branches within proximity of utility cables and residential structures.
  • Recharge all power banks, battery-powered radios, and portable emergency lamps.
  • Anchor outdoor water tanks, trash containers, and loose garden materials.
PHASE 2
DURING a Cyclone
  • Shelter in an interior room without external glass windows, bathrooms, or reinforced hallways.
  • Disconnect electrical appliances and turn off LPG gas regulators.
  • Eye of the Storm Trap: Do not venture outside when wind suddenly ceases; the opposite wall of the cyclone will strike with extreme velocity.
  • Keep listening to official meteorological advisories via battery radio or phone alerts.
PHASE 3
AFTER a Cyclone
  • Remain inside until government disaster managers broadcast that the cyclone has formally dissipated.
  • Beware of snapped electric cables suspended in puddles; treat all fallen wires as live.
  • Do not enter flooded underpasses or drive near coastal tide surge zones.
  • Help elderly and injured neighbors reach designated medical camps.
HILLSLOPE PROTOCOL
Landslide Warning Signs & Action
  • Warning Signs: Tilting trees, utility poles leaning downhill, new cracks in soil, suddenly muddy river water, or rumbling sounds that gradually increase.
  • Move away from the path of debris flow or landslide immediately. Run perpendicular to the direction of flow, never downhill in its path.
  • If escape is impossible, curl into a tight ball and protect your head with your arms or heavy blankets.
  • Avoid river valleys and low-lying canyon floors during heavy torrential monsoon rains.
OCEAN COASTAL PROTOCOL
Tsunami Warning Signs & Evacuation
  • Natural Warning: A strong earthquake near the coast or sudden ocean water receding and exposing the seabed is nature's tsunami siren.
  • Do not stay to watch! Run immediately inland towards high elevation ground (at least 100 feet above sea level or 2 miles inland).
  • Tsunamis are not a single wave but a series of destructive surges that can last for many hours.
  • Never board a ship or boat in shallow harbor waters during a tsunami warning.
Fire Extinguisher PASS Method
  • P - Pull the safety pin at the top of the extinguisher.
  • A - Aim low, pointing the nozzle at the base of the fire.
  • S - Squeeze the handle slowly and evenly.
  • S - Sweep the nozzle from side to side until flames are extinguished.
Building Evacuation
  • Crawl Low Under Smoke: Smoke and toxic superheated gases rise to the ceiling; breathable clean air is near the floor.
  • Feel door handles with the back of your hand before opening. If hot, do not open.
  • Never use elevators during a fire emergency; always utilize marked emergency fire stairs.
  • Once outside, never re-enter a burning structure under any circumstance.
Wildfire Defense
  • Maintain a 30-foot defensible perimeter around your residence by clearing dry brush and leaves.
  • Close all windows, roof vents, and doors to prevent burning ember infiltration.
  • Wear N95/P100 respirators to prevent hazardous particulate lung injury.
  • Evacuate along pre-planned escape corridors well before flames approach road margins.

Preventive Measures
  • Avoid direct solar exposure between 11:30 AM and 4:30 PM during heatwave alerts.
  • Hydrate continuously with water, ORS (oral rehydration salt), buttermilk, or coconut water.
  • Wear lightweight, breathable, light-colored cotton clothing and wide-brimmed hats.
  • Never leave young children, disabled persons, or pets unattended in closed motor vehicles.
Recognizing Heatstroke
  • High Body Temperature: Core body temperature exceeding 104°F (40°C).
  • Altered Mental State: Confusion, slurred speech, delirium, dizziness, or loss of consciousness.
  • Skin Flushed & Hot: Skin turns red and hot; sweating may completely stop.
  • Rapid heart rate and pounding pulse.
Heatstroke First Aid
  • Call emergency medical services (108 / 112) immediately.
  • Move victim to a cool, air-conditioned room or deep shaded area.
  • Apply ice packs or cold damp towels to neck, armpits, and groin where major blood vessels run.
  • Do not give oral fluids if the patient is semi-conscious or vomiting.
